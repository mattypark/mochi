# I Analyzed 100 Substack Writers Making $10K+/Month. 89 Broke This 'Rule'

Jessica Onne — *Slay with Finances* — Feb 08, 2026

I spent 9 months commenting on Notes daily, restacking generously, building
relationships. My engagement was great. My revenue was $0.

So I analyzed 100 Substack writers making over $10,000 per month. 89 of them
barely engaged compared to what I was doing.

## The Rule That's Keeping You Busy (But Broke)

"Engagement is everything. Comment daily. Restack often. Build relationships. Be
generous with your attention. Show up for your community."

I followed this advice religiously for 9 months: commented on 10-15 Notes every
single day, restacked content 3-4 times per week, replied to everyone who
engaged, built genuine relationships with other writers.

My engagement looked great: 17 likes per post, 4-12 comments, steady follower
growth. My revenue: $0. Not "building slowly." Just zero.

## The 89/11 Split

Out of 100 writers making $10K+/month:

**11** were high-engagement, daily-presence creators. They made money through
paid communities or courses sold to highly engaged audiences. For them,
engagement WAS the business model.

**89** barely engaged compared to what I was doing:

- Posted 2-4 times per week (not daily)
- Commented on 3-7 posts per week (not 10-15 per day)
- Restacked maybe once a week, if that
- Engaged strategically with influential voices or potential clients only

But here's what they DID do:

- **Razor-sharp positioning** — you could tell exactly what they did in one sentence
- **Made direct offers regularly** — about 1 in every 3-4 posts mentioned how to work with them
- **Created strategic content** — every post connected to something they sold
- **Talked to big enough markets** — 1-2% conversion = real money

The uncomfortable truth: most engagement is just productive procrastination.

## The 4 Things the Successful 89 Did Differently

### 1. They picked markets big enough to matter

Doesn't work: "brand strategists for female service providers over 40 in the
wellness space transitioning from corporate." Total addressable market: maybe
3,000 people. The math doesn't work.

Works: niche by the **problem you solve** or the **identity people aspire to**,
not by demographics. "Brand strategists for people who are great at what they do
but invisible online." Same expertise, 100x larger market.

**The test:** if 1% of your target audience became clients, would you have a
thriving business? If not, zoom out one level.

### 2. They had a clear "thing" people could describe

Doesn't work: sounding like everyone else. "3 lessons I learned about X." "Here's
what most people get wrong about Y." These formats work when they're genuinely
your insight. They fail when you're templating someone else's success.

Works: a specific lens people can articulate in one sentence.

- "She's the one who treats productivity like therapy"
- "He's the one who thinks most marketing is just anxiety with a CTA"
- "She's the one who approaches business strategy like it's relationship counseling"

**The test:** can someone describe your thing without using generic words like
"authentic," "strategic," or "transformational"? If not, you blend in.

How to find it: what advice in your industry annoys you? What do people
consistently get wrong? Your perspective is hiding in your irritations and your
"actually, it's simpler than that" moments.

### 3. They connected every insight to an offer

Doesn't work: posting valuable content for months without ever mentioning how
someone can work with you. That trains your audience to expect free content
forever.

Works: roughly 1 in every 3-4 posts mentions what you offer, who it's for, what
outcome it delivers, and how to get started.

A $15K/month writer's pattern:

1. Insight about a common problem
2. Case study of how they solved it for a client
3. Community engagement or educational content
4. "This is exactly what I do in [offer] — if you're struggling with [problem], let's fix it"

Your audience isn't annoyed by offers. They're confused by the absence of them.

### 4. They engaged strategically, not constantly

Doesn't work: 2-3 hours daily commenting for visibility.

Works: 20-30 minutes, 3 times per week, only on posts from potential clients,
posts from strategic voices, and conversations where you have genuine value to
add.

- From commenting for visibility → commenting for connection with the right people
- From restacking everyone → restacking only what reinforces your positioning
- From daily presence for the algorithm → strategic presence for humans who might buy

75 minutes per week instead of 12-18 hours.

## What changed, 8 weeks later

- Revenue: $0 → $12,000
- Engagement metrics: DOWN
- Client inquiries: 0-1/month → 6-9/month
- Hours on Substack: 15/week → 8/week

## Measure what matters

Stop tracking total likes, total comments, total followers. Start tracking client
inquiries, conversations with potential clients, and revenue.

## The nuance

If you're selling courses, paid communities, or products to creators, high
engagement might BE your business model — the 11 proved that works. If you're a
service provider, consultant, or coach, engagement is a supporting tactic, not
the main strategy.

Performative engagement feels virtuous. Strategic engagement converts.

## The fix

1. **Market size** — zoom out until the math works (1% conversion = real business)
2. **Positioning** — get so clear on your thing that people can describe it in one sentence
3. **Offers** — make it obvious how to work with you in 1 out of every 3-4 posts
4. **Engagement** — cut it to 75 minutes/week, make it strategic
